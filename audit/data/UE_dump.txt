6:  | WHAT IT DOES
7:  | Every order carries costs: the product, the shipping, the packaging, the payment fee, the returns. What's left is the most we can spend to win that order.
8:  | This sheet works out that figure for Nigeria, the UK, Canada and the US. It then compares it with what we actually paid in advertising for each order.
9:  | Paid less than we can afford? The country is making money. Scale it.
Paid more? Every order is losing money. Cut it.
11:  | HOW TO USE IT
12:  | 1.   Enter your costs. Fill in the yellow cells on the Unit Economics tab. Update them when supplier or courier prices change.
13:  | 2.   Choose a period. Last 30 days, last 90 days, year to date, or the last 12 months.
14:  | 3.   Read the verdict. Scale, Hold or Cut, one for each country.
16:  | THE FOUR NUMBERS THAT MATTER
17:  | Allowable CAC   The most we can pay for an order and still break even.
18:  | Target CAC   The most we can pay and still hit our profit target.
19:  | Actual CAC   What we really paid. Ad spend in the country ÷ orders shipped there.
20:  | Profit per order after ads   What we keep. Green means profit. Red means we lose money on every order.
22:  | WHERE THE NUMBERS COME FROM
23:  | Ad spend from Meta. Orders and order values from Shopify. Both refresh every morning. Costs come from you.
24:  | Actual CAC counts every order shipped to a country, including repeat customers and orders that did not come from an ad. Each order is judged on its own, with no repeat purchase assumed.
26:  | Data up to 7 October 2026
