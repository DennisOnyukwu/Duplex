# Ottawa rewrites the federal credit union rulebook

Between November 2023 and November 2026 almost every rulebook that governs a federal credit union changed, and the changes that matter most to Coast Capital fall into four groups: OSFI's new operational-risk guidelines and its November 19, 2026 draft capital relief for smaller institutions; the March 26, 2026 anti-money-laundering amendments that raised FINTRAC penalties 40-fold; a consumer-protection wave (the $10 NSF cap, OBSI as sole complaints body, a fraud regime planned for July 1, 2027); and new payments, open-banking, stablecoin and cyber statutes that are enacted but mostly not yet in force. The direction of travel split in two. Conduct and financial-crime rules got stricter and more expensive to breach, while prudential rules for small and medium-sized banks are being loosened in the name of competition. Three dates sit in the next eight months: OSFI Guideline E-23 on model risk (May 1, 2027), FCAC's revised complaint-handling guideline (June 23, 2027) and the Bank Act fraud provisions (planned July 1, 2027). Budget 2026, tabled November 5, 2026, proposes a second wave, including moving AML supervision of federally regulated institutions from FINTRAC to OSFI, raising deposit insurance to $250,000 and a $0 consumer liability cap for unauthorized transactions; none of it is law yet. To stay current, primary-source feeds (OSFI, FINTRAC, Bank of Canada, Canada Gazette, LEGISinfo) beat social media, LinkedIn is the only social channel worth daily attention, and Instagram carries almost no regulatory content. Some items below rest on law-firm summaries rather than the statute itself, and those are flagged where they affect a date or a dollar figure.

## OSFI finished its operational-risk build-out, then pivoted to competition

OSFI spent 2023 and 2024 adding non-financial risk guidelines and spent 2025 and 2026 trimming and recalibrating. Both halves bind Coast Capital, which OSFI classifies as a small and medium-sized bank (SMSB). Dates written as "Nov 1 / Jan 1" mean November 1 for October year-ends and January 1 for December year-ends.

### The draft 2028 capital guideline is the most favourable prudential change in the period

On November 19, 2026 OSFI launched its "Competitive Prudential Initiative" with a draft Capital Adequacy Requirements (CAR) Guideline for 2028, which it called "our most ambitious effort to date to level the playing field in Canadian banking" ([OSFI](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/news/osfi-launches-its-competitive-prudential-initiative-putting-resilience-work-support-greater)). For SMSBs, **including federal credit unions**, the draft removes the minimum and supervisory-target Tier 1 risk-based capital requirement and exempts them from the non-viability contingent capital requirement. It also cuts standardized risk weights: residential mortgages with a borrower credit score above 720 fall from 20% to 10% at loan-to-value below 50% and from 35% to 27.5% at 70–80%; SME corporate exposures fall from 75% to 65%; flat corporate from 100% to 80% ([OSFI backgrounder](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/news/backgrounder-draft-capital-adequacy-requirements-guideline-2028)). The one tightening measure applies 1.75 times the general residential risk weight to mortgages above 65% loan-to-value that could become negatively amortizing. **Comments close February 17, 2027; the proposed effective date is Nov 1, 2027 / Jan 1, 2028.** How much relief Coast Capital captures depends on the quality of its credit-score and property-income data, because income-producing residential property and sub-720 borrowers are excluded.

Other capital and liquidity instruments changed on an annual cycle. CAR (2026) took effect Nov 1, 2025 / Jan 1, 2026 with implementation guidance for combined loan plans and a clarification that mortgage classification affects capital, not underwriting ([OSFI](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/news/backgrounder-final-capital-adequacy-requirements-guideline-2026)). CAR (2027), final on September 10, 2026, takes effect **Nov 1, 2026 / Jan 1, 2027** with changes to SME and construction lending and a streamlined route for SMSBs seeking internal-ratings approval ([OSFI](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/news/backgrounder-final-capital-adequacy-requirements-guideline-2027)). The revised crypto-asset capital and liquidity guideline takes effect on the same dates ([OSFI](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/news/backgrounder-capital-liquidity-treatment-crypto-asset-exposures-banking-guideline-2027-1)). Liquidity rules targeting certain retail deposit categories took effect May 1, 2026, and a draft Internal Liquidity Adequacy Assessment Process guideline was issued May 21, 2026 ([OSFI Annual Risk Outlook](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/about-osfi/reports-publications/osfis-annual-risk-outlook-fiscal-year-2026-2027); [OSFI](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/news/osfis-quarterly-release-strengthening-resilience-while-reducing-complexity)). A final revised Guideline B-12 on interest rate risk was published September 10, 2026; its effective date was not on the release page ([OSFI](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/news/osfis-quarterly-release-resilience-enables-competition-innovation-sustainable-economic-growth-0)). A draft Guideline B-2 on large exposure limits for SMSBs is pending, and OSFI's own pages conflict on whether the final version arrives in February or May 2027 ([OSFI release](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/news/osfi-launches-its-competitive-prudential-initiative-putting-resilience-work-support-greater); [OSFI schedule](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/about-osfi/progress-our-initiatives/modernizing-policies-guidance-supervision-regulatory-efficiency/osfis-policy-releases-announcements-schedule)).

Two widely reported changes do not bind a standardized-approach credit union directly: the Basel III output floor, frozen at 67.5% "until further notice" since February 12, 2025, and the Domestic Stability Buffer, cut to 3.0% on June 19, 2026 ([OSFI](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/news/statement-superintendent-financial-institutions-basel-iii-standardized-capital-floor-level); [OSFI](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/osfi-lowers-domestic-stability-buffer-30-so-canadas-largest-banks-can-deploy-more-capital)). They matter as context: both eased constraints on the largest banks, and the draft CAR (2028) is OSFI's attempt to narrow the resulting gap from the SMSB side.

### Mortgage underwriting now carries three overlapping constraints

Guideline B-20 remains the operative underwriting guideline. The minimum qualifying rate for uninsured mortgages is unchanged at the greater of the contract rate plus 2% or 5.25%, but since **November 21, 2024** OSFI no longer prescribes it for uninsured straight switches between federally regulated lenders at renewal ([OSFI](https://osfi-bsif.gc.ca/Eng/fi-if/in-ai/Pages/mqr-nfo.aspx); [OSFI backgrounder](https://osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/news/backgrounder-minimum-qualifying-rate-mqr)). On top of borrower-level tests sits a confidential, institution-specific **loan-to-income limit** on the share of new uninsured mortgages above 4.5 times income, in effect since each institution's fiscal 2025 and confirmed as staying in place on January 29, 2026, with quarterly reporting ([OSFI](https://osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/financial-institutions/banks/loan-income-limits-uninsured-mortgage-portfolios)). The third layer is the capital classification rules in CAR. OSFI plans to fold B-20 into a single principles-based Credit Risk Management Guideline; draft chapters were deferred to February 12, 2027, so replacement is a 2027–2028 event at the earliest ([OSFI](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/guidance/guidance-library/letter-industry-updates-osfis-policy-plan)). On the Department of Finance side, the insured-mortgage price cap rose to $1.5 million and 30-year insured amortizations were extended to all first-time buyers and all new-build purchasers on December 15, 2024 ([Department of Finance](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/12/boldest-mortgage-reforms-in-decades-come-into-force-today.html)).

OSFI's structural posture changed too. Since August 2024 it publishes guidance on four quarterly release days, each followed two weeks later by an Industry Day; the next are December 3, 2026 (Industry Day) and February 12, 2027 (release) ([OSFI schedule](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/about-osfi/progress-our-initiatives/modernizing-policies-guidance-supervision-regulatory-efficiency/osfis-policy-releases-announcements-schedule)). It rescinded 20 guidelines and advisories from April 1, 2025 and made the review ongoing ([OSFI](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/news/backgrounder-results-osfis-policy-review)). Budget 2026 proposes giving OSFI a statutory secondary competition objective and letting provincial credit unions acquire federal ones; these are proposals, not enacted law ([McCarthy Tétrault](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/techlex/budget-2026-financial-institutions-update)).

## Financial-crime penalties rose 40-fold and the supervisor itself is set to change

The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) was amended in three waves. The first two are in force. The third is still before Parliament or only announced.

### What is in force today

**Bill C-12** (Strengthening Canada's Immigration System and Borders Act) and **Bill C-15** (the Budget 2025 implementation act) both received Royal Assent on **March 26, 2026**, and most of their PCMLTFA changes took effect that day ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/03/canada-aml-update-three-bills-make-major-amendments-to-the-pcmltfa)). Maximum administrative penalties are now **$40,000 for minor, $4 million for serious and $20 million for very serious violations**, with a per-notice cap of the greater of $20 million or 3% of gross global revenue ([Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/canada-s-new-anti-money-laundering-legislation-significantly-raises-penalties-and-adds-new-violation/)). A compliance program must now be "reasonably designed, risk-based and effective", and every compliance-program failing (policies, risk assessment, effectiveness testing) moved from serious to very serious ([FINTRAC](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/businesses-entreprises/changes-changements-eng)). Every penalty now ends in a mandatory compliance agreement, and refusing or breaching one leads to a public compliance order. Secondary sources disagree on the maximum for breaching an order: DLA Piper gives the greater of $30 million or 3% of global revenue, Torys gives $20 million or 3%; the statute should be checked ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/03/canada-aml-update-three-bills-make-major-amendments-to-the-pcmltfa); [Torys](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/torys-quarterly/q4-2026/canadas-aml-landscape)). FINTRAC's head now sits on the Financial Institutions Supervisory Committee alongside OSFI, the Bank of Canada, CDIC, FCAC and Finance, and can exchange supervisory information with them. Violations entirely before March 26, 2026 stay under the old scale, and FINTRAC scopes each examination period to fall within one framework ([FINTRAC](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/pen/3-eng)).

The earlier wave (Bills C-47, C-59 and C-69 and their regulations) added reporting duties that apply directly to a credit union:

|---|---|---|

Three adjacent changes complete the in-force picture. The federal registry of individuals with significant control has been public since **January 22, 2024**, which is what the discrepancy-reporting duty checks against ([Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2024/public-access-to-isc-information-under-the-cbca/)). The 2023 "deemed ownership" rule in sanctions law treats property of an entity controlled by a sanctioned person as that person's property, so name screening alone no longer suffices ([Fasken](https://www.fasken.com/en/knowledge/2023/08/significant-amendments-to-canadas-sanctions-laws)). Bill C-69 added Criminal Code orders requiring an institution to keep an account open or active and repeating production orders that can reach PCMLTFA report copies, which need an intake process that overrides exit decisions for affected accounts ([Department of Justice](https://justice.gc.ca/eng/trans/bm-mb/other-autre/c69/division34c-section34c.html)). The criminal interest rate also fell to **35% APR on January 1, 2025**, with commercial loans from $10,000 to $500,000 capped at 48% ([Bennett Jones](https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Blogs/Lower-Criminal-Interest-Rate-Now-in-Effect)).

### Enforcement has already reached credit unions

FINTRAC dropped its "last resort" language on penalties in an August 2025 policy revision and issued **35 notices of violation totalling over $247 million in 2025/26**, its highest ever ([Torys](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/torys-quarterly/q4-2026/canadas-aml-landscape)). Six credit unions or caisses were penalized between June 2025 and September 2026, including **$676,500 against Caisse populaire acadienne (UNI Financial Cooperation), a federally regulated credit union**, published September 24, 2026 ([FINTRAC](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/pen/4-eng)). The recurring findings are a ready-made effectiveness checklist: unfiled suspicious transaction reports, unreported incoming international transfers of $10,000 or more (Cambrian, $116,160), weak monitoring of high-risk clients (Synergy, $214,500) and clients rated standard risk that should have been elevated (VersaBank) ([FINTRAC](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/new-neuf/nr/2025-06-10-eng); [FINTRAC](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/new-neuf/nr/2025-11-27-1-eng); [FINTRAC](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/new-neuf/nr/2026-05-05-eng)). All of those were assessed on the pre-March 2026 scale. The Financial Action Task Force's evaluation of Canada, published September 29, 2026, rated supervision of financial institutions only partially compliant and placed Canada in regular follow-up with a three-year progress report, which points to rising supervisory intensity through about 2029 ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/canadas-2026-fatf-mutual-evaluation)).

Budget 2026 proposes the next round: a **$0 liability cap for unauthorized transactions** on all account types absent gross negligence; a Multi-Sector Anti-Fraud Framework spanning financial institutions, telecoms and digital platforms; a ban on investment-account transfer fees (draft regulations published November 7, 2026); an annual fee statement; branch-closure duties; and **deposit insurance of $250,000**, with unlimited coverage for eligible registered and tax-free deposits ([Torys](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2026/11/budget-2026-financial-services-and-banking); [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/2026-federal-budget-financial-sector-highlights/)). The fraud measures would shift loss allocation toward institutions before telecoms and platforms carry equivalent duties.

Provincial law reaches Coast Capital mainly through subsidiaries and non-banking activities. BC's Mortgage Services Act replaced the Mortgage Brokers Act on **October 13, 2026**, and BC's subscription and auto-renewal consumer amendments took effect **August 1, 2026** ([BCFSA](https://www.bcfsa.ca/industry-resources/mortgage-broker-resources/msa-landing-page/mortgage-services-act-rules-regulation); [McMillan](https://mcmillan.ca/insights/publications/consumer-protection-updates-in-british-columbia-key-compliance-considerations-for-businesses-ahead-of-august-1-2026/)). How each provision applies to the federal credit union itself needs legal confirmation.

## Conclusion

The pattern across these three years is a trade: Ottawa is lowering the capital and entry cost of being a small federal deposit-taker while raising the cost of conduct and financial-crime failures. For a federal credit union that trade is favourable on balance, but only if the compliance function keeps pace, because the relief (draft CAR 2028, easier amalgamations, higher deposit insurance) is mostly still proposed while the exposure (40-fold AML penalties, a "very serious" label on program weaknesses, OSFI's more frequent penalties) is already law. The convergence of supervisors compounds this. FINTRAC already shares supervisory information with OSFI, and Budget 2026 would hand OSFI the AML file outright, so an AML finding, a fraud-control gap and a model-governance weakness will increasingly be read together as one judgment about the institution.

The practical implication for a regulatory advisory role is that status tracking has become the core skill. A large share of what was "passed" in 2026 is not in force, several firm-looking dates still depend on an order or final regulation that has not been published, and Coast Capital's own profile changed with its 2026 combination with Prospera and Sunshine Coast credit unions ([Coast Capital](https://coastcapitalsavings.com/about-us/our-leadership-team)). A monitoring routine built on the Gazette, LEGISinfo and the regulators' own feeds catches those status changes on the day they happen; commentary and social media explain them a week or two later.
