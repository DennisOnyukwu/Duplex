## 1. Bank Act / Financial Consumer Protection Framework amendments since late 2023

### Takeaway
The big enacted package is Bill C-15 (Budget Implementation Act, 2025, No. 1; S.C. 2026, c. 3; Royal Assent 26 March 2026), whose Bank Act consumer provisions (customer-targeted fraud, cheque access) mostly await an order in council — the fraud regime is planned for 1 July 2027. The $10 NSF fee cap has been in force since 12 March 2026, OBSI has been sole external complaints body since 1 Nov 2024, and Budget 2026 (5 Nov 2026) proposes a second wave ($0 liability for unauthorized transactions, branch-closure duties, annual fee statement, investment-account transfer fee ban, Multi-Sector Anti-Fraud Framework), none of it enacted yet.

### Cited Findings

**1a. Single external complaints body (OBSI) — IN FORCE**
- OBSI assumed responsibility as Canada's single external complaints body (ECB) for federally regulated banks on 1 November 2024 — [FCAC news release, Oct 2024](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/news/2024/10/canadians-now-have-a-single-external-complaints-body-for-banking.html)
- The designation was announced in October 2023 — [FCAC designation announcement](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/news/2023/10/designation-dun-organisme-externe-de-traitement-des-plaintes-unique-pour-le-secteur-bancaire-au-canada.html)
- Consumers can go to OBSI if not satisfied by the bank's internal process or if the bank has not closed/resolved the complaint within 56 days — [FCAC statement, July 2026](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/news/2026/07/fcac-statement-on-the-independent-evaluation-of-the-external-complaint-body-for-banking.html)
- First independent evaluation of OBSI as sole banking ECB: evaluator (CRK) appointed Jan 2026, evaluation began Feb 2026, report published 22 July 2026; FCAC says the report notes OBSI has met regulatory requirements and improved since the 2021 evaluation, and recommends progressive improvements — [FCAC statement](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/news/2026/07/fcac-statement-on-the-independent-evaluation-of-the-external-complaint-body-for-banking.html); [OBSI release](https://www.obsi.ca/en/news/posts/2026-independent-external-review-of-obsi-s-operations-released/); [report PDF](https://www.obsi.ca/media/wiobo33n/2026-independent-evaluation-of-obsi.pdf)

**1b. NSF fee cap — IN FORCE (12 March 2026)**
- Regulations Amending the Financial Consumer Protection Framework Regulations, SOR/2025-96, registered 12 March 2025, published Canada Gazette Part II, Vol. 159, No. 7 (26 March 2025) — [Canada Gazette Part II](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-03-26/html/sor-dors96-eng.html)
- In force 12 March 2026: NSF fees on personal deposit accounts capped at $10 (down from as much as $50); no more than one NSF fee within two business days on the same account; no NSF fee where the shortfall/overdraft is under $10 — [Department of Finance release, 11 Mar 2026](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/03/minister-champagne-announces-new-10-cap-on-nsf-fees-and-other-measures-to-lower-banking-costs-for-canadians.html); [FCAC release, Mar 2026](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/news/2026/03/new-nsf-fee-regulations-bring-down-cost-of-banking-for-canadians.html)
- Applies only to federally regulated banks and federal credit unions under the Bank Act (79 institutions per Finance); FCAC oversees compliance — [Department of Finance release](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/03/minister-champagne-announces-new-10-cap-on-nsf-fees-and-other-measures-to-lower-banking-costs-for-canadians.html)
- Pre-publication was in Canada Gazette Part I on 16 Nov 2024 — [Canada Gazette Part I](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2024/2024-11-16/html/reg1-eng.html)

**1c. Low-cost / no-cost accounts — IN FORCE as a voluntary public commitment (not regulation)**
- Modernized Commitment on Low-Cost and No-Cost Accounts fully in place 1 December 2025: low-cost account at no more than $4/month with up to 50% more debit transactions (including e-Transfers); $0 accounts for youth, students, GIS-recipient seniors, RDSP beneficiaries and (per secondary source) newcomers in their first year — [FCAC commitment page](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/industry/laws-regulations/low-cost-no-cost-accounts.html); [Department of Finance release](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/03/minister-champagne-announces-new-10-cap-on-nsf-fees-and-other-measures-to-lower-banking-costs-for-canadians.html)
- Signatories as of 31 March 2026 (per search summary of FCAC's 2025–26 annual report): Alterna Bank, Bank of China (Canada), BMO, CIBC, Hana Bank Canada, ICICI Bank, ICBC, Innovation Federal Credit Union, Laurentian, National Bank, RBC, Scotiabank, Tangerine, TD — [FCAC Annual Report 2025–26](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/corporate/planning/annual-reports/annual-report-2025-2026.html)

**1d. Bill C-15 — Budget Implementation Act, 2025, No. 1 (S.C. 2026, c. 3) — Royal Assent 26 March 2026**
- Bill introduced 18 Nov 2025 following the 4 Nov 2025 budget; Royal Assent 26 March 2026 — [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-ca/knowledge/publications/e89206f0/bill-c-15-proposes-amendments-to-competition-act); [MLT Aikins](https://www.mltaikins.com/insights/federal-government-narrows-scope-of-the-competition-acts-anti-greenwashing-provisions-as-bill-c-15-receives-royal-assent/)
- **Customer-targeted fraud (Part 5, Division 16) — PASSED, NOT IN FORCE; planned 1 July 2027.** Adds provisions to Part XII.2 of the Bank Act: fraud policies and procedures, consumer ability to turn off/adjust account capabilities, fraud data reporting to FCAC, annual reporting by institutions and the FCAC Commissioner. In force on a day fixed by order in council — [DLA Piper bulletin (Legal 500 reprint; page returned 404 on fetch, content per search summary)](https://www.legal500.com/developments/press-releases/changes-under-bill-c-15-affecting-the-bank-act/)
- **Implementing regulations — PROPOSED (Canada Gazette Part I, 27 June 2026; 30-day comment period):** Regulations Amending the Financial Consumer Protection Framework Regulations. Key content — [Canada Gazette Part I, 27 June 2026](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg2-eng.html):
  - Express consent required before enabling any capability that transfers funds electronically (wire transfers, global money transfers, Interac e-Transfer), after disclosure of the capability's nature/uses and verification of the account holder; consent per activation. Exempt: transfers between a person's own accounts at the same institution, ATM withdrawals, payment-card credential payments, pre-authorized debits, direct bill payments. Capabilities already enabled on existing accounts are grandfathered.
  - Consumers must be able to disable capabilities and adjust transaction limits; a requested limit increase takes effect immediately only if the requester is verified as the account holder, otherwise next business day.
  - Policies and procedures must set criteria for investigating suspicious transactions and for notifying customers of suspicious capability/limit requests; effectiveness review at least annually.
  - Annual fraud report to FCAC Commissioner on each fraud instance (confirmed or alleged, attempted or completed) with 13 data points (type, tactic, channel, method, amounts, reimbursement, authorized vs unauthorized, whether delayed/stopped, victim age range, gender, first three postal-code characters), plus training and fraud-reduction targets; due within 135 days of calendar year-end. FCAC reports confidentially to the Minister by 30 September each year.
  - Account-opening disclosure of which capabilities need consent, can be disabled, or have adjustable limits.
  - Proposed coming into force 1 July 2027 (same day as the Bank Act provisions, by order in council); first fraud report due 15 May 2029 covering calendar 2028.
  - Bank Act sections referenced: 627.131(1), 627.132(1) and (3), 627.134(2)–(4), 627.135(1); regulation-making authority 627.998(a), (p), (q), (s)–(v).
- **Cheque access (Part 5, Division 15) — PASSED; in-force status NOT VERIFIED.** Clause 331 amends Bank Act s. 627.22 to raise the amount available for immediate withdrawal after a cheque deposit from $100 to $150, and removes the timing distinction between in-person and other deposit methods — [Library of Parliament legislative summary (403 on fetch; per search summary)](https://lop.parl.ca/sites/PublicWebsite/default/en_CA/ResearchPublications/LegislativeSummaries/451C15E); [Bill C-15 first reading text](https://www.parl.ca/documentviewer/en/45-1/bill/C-15/first-reading). **Conflict:** a machine summary of the Department of Finance 11 March 2026 release returned "$100 to $250"; the bill text and Library of Parliament say $150 — treat $150 as correct pending check of the consolidated Act — [Department of Finance release](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/03/minister-champagne-announces-new-10-cap-on-nsf-fees-and-other-measures-to-lower-banking-costs-for-canadians.html)
- **Cheque hold periods — PROPOSED (regulations under development, no date):** government is developing regulations to shorten hold periods; current Access to Funds Regulations (SOR/2012-24) remain the operative rules — [Department of Finance release](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/03/minister-champagne-announces-new-10-cap-on-nsf-fees-and-other-measures-to-lower-banking-costs-for-canadians.html); [Access to Funds Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2012-24/page-1.html)
- **Bank Act sunset — PASSED.** Division 10 of Part 5 extends the period during which federal financial institutions may carry on business (new date not captured — see Gaps) — [Library of Parliament summary (per search summary)](https://lop.parl.ca/sites/PublicWebsite/default/en_CA/ResearchPublications/LegislativeSummaries/451C15E)
- **Federal credit union-specific amendments (as proposed in the bill; Torys bulletin Nov 2025)** — [Torys](https://www.torys.com/en/our-latest-thinking/publications/2025/11/budget-2025-implementation-federal-financial-institutions):
  - A federal credit union (FCU) resulting from continuance (or continuance plus amalgamation) may seek ministerial transitional relief from certain Financial Consumer Protection Framework provisions for up to three years from letters patent, subject to a compliance plan.
  - FCU–provincial credit union amalgamation: no special resolution of FCU members needed where the provincial credit union's assets are 25% or less of the FCU's; FCU directors' resolution plus special resolutions of the provincial credit union's members/shareholders; by-laws and head-office province must be unchanged; meeting still required if requested by the greater of two voting members or 1% of voting members; agreement must be disclosed to all members.
  - FCU may purchase all or substantially all assets of a provincial credit union for cash/securities; ministerial approval within 45 days (extendable by 45) of a complete application; transitional relief and corresponding CDIC Act deposit relief; acquired credit union's directors may be added until the next annual meeting. Torys notes provincial statutes need matching amendments and no Income Tax Act rollover was provided.
  - Motor-vehicle leasing permitted, with ministerial approval, for a continued FCU that did it before continuance.
  - Notice-and-access for member/shareholder meeting materials available to FCUs.
  - Repeal of statutory portfolio limits (real property, equities), replaced by Superintendent direction power; public-holding (35% float) equity threshold raised from $2B to $4B.
- **Consumer-driven banking:** C-15 completed the legislative framework and gave the Bank of Canada (not FCAC) regulatory and implementation responsibility — see section 2.

**1e. Budget 2026 (tabled 5 November 2026) — ALL PROPOSED unless noted** — [Torys](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2026/11/budget-2026-financial-services-and-banking); [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/2026-federal-budget-financial-sector-highlights/)
- **Unauthorized transactions:** Bank Act amendments to cap consumer liability at $0 for unauthorized transactions across all bank account types absent gross negligence (extending the credit-card rule). Blakes describes this as a consultation; Torys lists it as a proposal.
- **Multi-Sector Anti-Fraud Framework:** legislation imposing common duties on financial institutions, telecoms and digital platforms to prevent, detect, disrupt and respond to fraud and cooperate across sectors; draft legislation to be consulted on first; part of the National Anti-Fraud Strategy.
- **Investment account transfers — PROPOSED REGULATIONS (Canada Gazette, 7 Nov 2026):** Regulations Amending the Financial Consumer Protection Framework Regulations (Investment Account Transfers) would prohibit transfer/closing fees (typically ~$150 per account) unless the request is within 90 days of first funding; asset/cash details within two clearing days, impediments flagged within two more, settlement within 10 clearing days; deposit-type instruments and PPNs re-registered within three business days where terms allow; public disclosure of transfer process, costs, timelines and complaint procedures — [Torys](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2026/11/budget-2026-financial-services-and-banking)
- **Annual fee statement:** regulations to require a single annual statement of fees on chequing and savings accounts (EU/UK-style); no date.
- **Branch closures:** Bank Act amendments to require online posting of closure notices and fee waivers for closing/transferring affected accounts during the prescribed period.
- **Remote account opening:** banks must authenticate government-issued ID, consistent with in-person requirements.
- **Redress:** confirm consumer entitlement to redress where interest is unpaid or incorrectly credited (expanding Bank Act s. 627.977, which currently covers unauthorized fees/penalties).
- **Credit card payment allocation:** consultation on requiring payments above the minimum to go first to the highest-interest balance (Québec model).
- **Code of Conduct for the Prevention of Economic Abuse:** announced in Budget 2025; under negotiation with banks; would cover training, safeguards, warning signs, escalation, public reporting, with FCAC oversight.
- **Federal credit unions:** Bank Act amendment giving qualifying cooperative shareholders more flexibility to invest in and control an FCU, subject to ministerial approval; Finance Minister policy statement on using ministerial tools to support entry, including credit union continuance.
- **Competition measures:** OSFI secondary competition objective; one-year statutory decision timeline for new-entrant applications; widely-held threshold raised from $12B to $25B equity.
- **AML supervision:** transfer of AML/ATF supervision of federally regulated financial institutions from FINTRAC to OSFI (no date).
- Other firm summaries: [McCarthy Tétrault](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/techlex/budget-2026-financial-institutions-update); [Fasken](https://www.fasken.com/en/knowledge/2026/11/2026-federal-budget-highlights-affecting-financial-services); [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en-ca/insights-resources/articles/2026/budget-2026-what-it-means-for-canadian-financial-services)

**1f. Financial crime bills touching conduct**
- Bill C-29 (Financial Crimes Agency): introduced April 2026; second reading and referral to committee 18 June 2026; at House committee stage per LEGISinfo — **PROPOSED** — [LEGISinfo C-29](https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/45-1/c-29); [Osler](https://www.osler.com/en/insights/blogs/risk/canada-takes-aim-financial-crime-bill-c-29-financial-crimes-agency/)
- Bill S-8, Combatting Financial Crimes and Drug Trafficking Act, introduced 3 Nov 2026: banks may accept cash deposits only from the account holder or an authorized person (prescribed exceptions); offence for businesses accepting $10,000+ in cash — **PROPOSED** — [Torys](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2026/11/budget-2026-financial-services-and-banking)
- Bill C-12 described by Blakes as passed, with stronger FINTRAC enforcement and penalty powers (Royal Assent date not verified) — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/2026-federal-budget-financial-sector-highlights/)
- National Anti-Fraud Strategy: announced October 2025 with the Financial Crimes Agency; described in spring 2026 as still under development by Finance — [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en-ca/insights-resources/articles/2026/canada-new-approach-to-financial-crime)

### Inferences
- Coast Capital should plan on 1 July 2027 as the working compliance date for express-consent/capability-disable/transaction-limit controls and fraud policies, with data capture ready by 1 January 2028 for the first report (due 15 May 2029). The final regulations and the order in council were not located, so the dates remain "planned".
- Coast Capital does not appear in the signatory list for the modernized low-cost/no-cost account commitment as summarized (Innovation FCU is the only credit union listed); worth confirming directly against the FCAC page.
- The three-year transitional relief and the simplified amalgamation / asset-purchase routes are the measures most directly relevant to an existing FCU looking to grow by combining with provincial credit unions; whether these specific C-15 clauses are already in force was not confirmed.
- Budget 2026's $0-liability rule plus the Multi-Sector Anti-Fraud Framework would shift fraud loss allocation materially toward institutions; neither has a bill yet.

### Gaps
- New Bank Act sunset date after C-15 Division 10: not captured (Library of Parliament page blocked). Check Bank Act s. 21.
- Whether C-15's s. 627.22 change ($150 immediate access) and the FCU amalgamation/asset-purchase clauses are in force as of Nov 2026: not verified.
- Whether the June 2026 fraud regulations were finalized in Canada Gazette Part II: no Part II publication found.
- No Budget 2026 implementation bill number found (budget is three weeks old).
- Budget 2024 (Bill C-69, Budget Implementation Act, 2024, No. 1) and Fall Economic Statement 2023 (Bill C-59) Bank Act items were not separately researched this session; no sourced findings.

## 2. FCAC guidelines, mandate and supervisory priorities

### Takeaway
The one new FCAC instrument in the window is the revised Guideline on Complaint-Handling Procedures (published 23 June 2026, effective 23 June 2027). The mortgage-relief guideline of July 2023 remains in place with quarterly reporting, and oversight of consumer-driven banking was moved from FCAC to the Bank of Canada by Bill C-15.

### Cited Findings
- **Revised Guideline on Complaint-Handling Procedures for Banks and Authorized Foreign Banks — PUBLISHED, NOT YET EFFECTIVE.** Original 27 Jan 2022; revised 23 June 2026; effective 23 June 2027. Makes explicit that banks may not pause the 56-day period; adds clear acknowledgement of complaints, a Notice of Final Decision, and an explicit expectation of a comprehensive policy for timely redress where required under the Bank Act — [FCAC guideline page](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/industry/commissioner-guidance/complaint-handling-procedures-banks.html); [FCAC news release, June 2026](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/news/2026/06/financial-consumer-agency-of-canada-publishes-revised-guideline-on-complaint-handling-procedures-for-banks.html); [McCarthy Tétrault (429 on fetch; per search summary)](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/techlex/how-fcac-s-revised-complaint-handling-guideline-raises-expectations-for-banks)
- **Guideline on Existing Consumer Mortgage Loans in Exceptional Circumstances — IN FORCE.** Issued 5 July 2023; covers natural persons with an existing residential mortgage on a principal residence who are under severe financial stress from exceptional circumstances and at risk of default; expects individualized relief measures as soon as feasible — [FCAC guideline](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/industry/commissioner-guidance/mortgage-loans-exceptional-circumstances.html)
- FCAC's supervisory highlight says institutions report quarterly under the guideline and have enhanced hardship programs or created supplementary relief programs (page date not captured) — [FCAC supervisory highlight](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/programs/research/guideline-mortgage-loans.html)
- **Guideline on Appropriate Products and Services — IN FORCE, unchanged in window.** Published 24 Feb 2022, effective 30 June 2022 — [FCAC guideline](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/industry/commissioner-guidance/guideline-appropriate-products-services-banks.html)
- **Consumer-driven banking:** Bill C-15 (Royal Assent 26 March 2026) gave the Bank of Canada regulatory and implementation responsibility for the framework — [Bank of Canada discussion note, May 2026](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/discussion-note-deliverables-dependencies-may-12-2026.pdf.pdf)
- Draft Phase 1 regulations under the Consumer-Driven Banking Act pre-published 27 June 2026 (60-day consultation); Bank of Canada consultation on draft supervisory guidelines for accreditation opened 2 Nov 2026; Phase 1 launch still awaited — **PROPOSED** — [Torys](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2026/11/budget-2026-financial-services-and-banking)
- **FCAC powers — PROPOSED (Budget 2026):** amend the FCAC Act so the higher administrative monetary penalty can apply to contraventions that began before April 2020 and continued afterward (responding to Community Trust Company, 2026 FC 58); clarify no ministerial approval needed for information-sharing agreements with provincial enforcement authorities; reflect FCAC's complaint-handling role — [Torys](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2026/11/budget-2026-financial-services-and-banking); [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/2026-federal-budget-financial-sector-highlights/)
- FCAC will receive annual fraud data from banks and report to the Minister under the proposed fraud regulations — [Canada Gazette Part I, 27 June 2026](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg2-eng.html)
- FCAC appeared before the House Finance Committee on 13 April 2026 (briefing package public) — [FCAC briefing package](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/corporate/transparency/briefing-packages-parliamentary-committee-appearances/standing-committee-finance-april-13-2026.html)

### Inferences
- The complaint-handling revision is the nearest dated FCAC deliverable for policy/procedure updates (23 June 2027), a week before the planned fraud regime date (1 July 2027).
- FCAC's remit is growing on fraud data, NSF enforcement and (proposed) economic-abuse code oversight, while it lost the open-banking supervisory role it was given in 2024.

### Gaps
- No evidence found of a 2024–2026 revision to the mortgage exceptional-circumstances guideline. One secondary source (FSRA Ontario page) lists an "effective date of June 19, 2024" for it — unconfirmed and possibly a page-date artifact.
- FCAC supervisory priorities for 2026–27 and any new/updated whistleblowing guideline: not found in searches.
- Full list of changes in the revised complaint-handling guideline not read from the primary page.
- Whether participation in consumer-driven banking is mandatory for an institution of Coast Capital's size, and the Phase 1 go-live date: not confirmed.

## 3. Criminal interest rate

### Takeaway
The criminal rate of interest dropped to 35% APR on 1 January 2025 with regulations carving out commercial and pawn loans; Budget 2026 now proposes tightening the payday-loan exemption (42-day minimum term, instalments, no credit insurance).

### Cited Findings
- 35% APR criminal rate IN FORCE 1 January 2025 (previously 60% effective annual rate, about 48% APR) — [Bennett Jones](https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Blogs/Lower-Criminal-Interest-Rate-Now-in-Effect)
- Criminal Interest Rate Regulations, SOR/2024-114, published Canada Gazette Part II on 19 June 2024 — [Canada Gazette Part II](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2024/2024-06-19/html/sor-dors114-eng.html)
- Exemptions: commercial loans over $10,000 up to $500,000 capped at 48% APR; commercial loans above $500,000 uncapped; pawn loans under $1,000 capped at 48% APR — [Torys](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2024/01/criminal-interest-rate-reduced-to-crack-down-on-predatory-lending); [Bennett Jones](https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Blogs/Lower-Criminal-Interest-Rate-Now-in-Effect)
- Payday loans: cost of borrowing capped at $14 per $100 borrowed — [MoneySense](https://www.moneysense.ca/save/debt/loans/canada-new-rules-for-high-interest-payday-loan/)
- **PROPOSED (Budget 2026):** amend Criminal Code s. 347.1 payday exemption to require a minimum 42-day term, repayment in instalments and no credit-insurance charges; provinces would need to align — [Torys](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2026/11/budget-2026-financial-services-and-banking); [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/2026-federal-budget-financial-sector-highlights/)

### Inferences
- Direct exposure for a credit union's own lending is low, but APR must be computed on the Criminal Code definition of interest (fees and charges included), which matters for small-dollar and fee-heavy products and for any fintech/partner lending.

### Gaps
- Outcome of the Department of Finance consultation on lowering the rate below 35%: no update found.
- A CBC article gives the commercial band as $10,000–$50,000; that conflicts with the Gazette/law-firm figure of $500,000 and appears to be an error — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7426411)

## 4. Department of Finance mortgage rules

### Takeaway
The insured-mortgage price cap rose to $1.5M and 30-year insured amortizations were extended to all first-time buyers and all new-build purchasers on 15 December 2024; Budget 2026 adds transparency measures rather than new eligibility changes.

### Cited Findings
- 1 August 2024: 30-year insured amortizations for first-time buyers purchasing new builds — [Department of Finance, Sept 2024](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/09/government-announces-boldest-mortgage-reforms-in-decades-to-unlock-homeownership-for-more-canadians.html)
- 15 December 2024 (IN FORCE): insured-mortgage price cap raised from $1M to $1.5M (first increase since 2012); 30-year insured amortizations for all first-time buyers and all buyers of new builds — [Department of Finance, 15 Dec 2024](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/12/boldest-mortgage-reforms-in-decades-come-into-force-today.html); [technical details release](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2024/09/government-announces-mortgage-reform-details-to-ensure-canadians-can-access-lower-monthly-mortgage-payments-by-december-15.html)
- **PROPOSED (Budget 2026, per Blakes):** Bank of Canada regulations to publish insured-mortgage rate information; a CRA income-verification service for mortgage lenders and insurers; consultation on prepayment-penalty transparency. Blakes also lists the $1.5M price limit and 30-year amortizations among the budget's mortgage items (these appear to restate the 2024 changes) — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/2026-federal-budget-financial-sector-highlights/)

### Inferences
- Prepayment-penalty transparency and a CRA income-verification service would both land in mortgage origination and disclosure processes; neither has draft text.

### Gaps
- Secondary-suite insured refinancing (announced for 15 January 2025), the Canadian Mortgage Charter (Fall Economic Statement 2023, updated Budget 2024) and the removal of the stress test on uninsured straight switches at renewal were not verified this session — no sourced findings; a follow-up on canada.ca and OSFI is needed.

## 5. CDIC

### Takeaway
The new Differential Premiums By-law (SOR/2025-165) came into force 29 April 2026 with semi-annual classification, and an amended Deposit Insurance Information By-law is proposed for 1 December 2026; Budget 2026 proposes raising coverage to $250,000 — the limit today is still $100,000 per category.

### Cited Findings

## 6. Competition Act amendments

### Takeaway
The 2024 greenwashing provisions were narrowed by Bill C-15 in March 2026, but the product-claim provision and the expanded private right of action (since 20 June 2025) still apply to financial marketing.

### Cited Findings
- Bill C-59 greenwashing provisions in force 20 June 2024; expanded private access to the Competition Tribunal in force 20 June 2025 — [BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/01/proposed-amendments-to-canadas-greenwashing-rules); [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-ca/knowledge/publications/e89206f0/bill-c-15-proposes-amendments-to-competition-act)
- Bill C-15 (Royal Assent 26 March 2026; changes in force on assent per MLT Aikins) removed the "internationally recognized methodology" requirement from para. 74.01(1)(b.2) (business/brand environmental claims), which must still be adequately substantiated with the onus on the claimant, and removed private-party access to the Tribunal for (b.2) claims — [MLT Aikins](https://www.mltaikins.com/insights/federal-government-narrows-scope-of-the-competition-acts-anti-greenwashing-provisions-as-bill-c-15-receives-royal-assent/); [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2026/legislation-passes-to-amend-environmental-claims-prohibitions-of-the-competition-act)
- Private parties can still bring applications under the product-specific provision 74.01(1)(b.1) and the general misleading-representation provision 74.01(1)(a) — [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-ca/knowledge/publications/e89206f0/bill-c-15-proposes-amendments-to-competition-act)
- Competition Bureau environmental-claims guidelines were issued 5 June 2025 and have been updated for the C-15 amendments — [MLT Aikins](https://www.mltaikins.com/insights/competition-bureau-provides-anti-greenwashing-clarity/)

### Inferences
- Sustainability claims about products (e.g. "green" mortgages, ESG funds) and any net-zero statements still need documented substantiation; private litigation exposure remains for product and general misleading claims.

### Gaps
- Drip-pricing provisions and Bill C-56 (Dec 2023) changes were not verified this session — no sourced findings.
