# Canadian AML / ATF / Sanctions / Financial-Crime Law Changes, Nov 2023 – Nov 2026 (federal credit union lens)

Research date: 2026-11-26. Status labels used throughout: **IN FORCE**, **PASSED – NOT IN FORCE**, **PROPOSED** (bill tabled or budget announcement only). Items not confirmed against a primary source current to late 2026 are flagged "UNVERIFIED" or moved to Gaps. Coast Capital Savings is a federal credit union, so it is a "financial entity" reporting entity under the PCMLTFA, a federally regulated financial institution (FRFI) supervised by OSFI, and a "Canadian financial institution" for sanctions purposes.

---

## 1. PCMLTFA statutory amendments (Bills C-47, C-59, C-69, C-2, C-12, C-15, S-8, Budget 2026)

### Takeaway
Three waves matter: (a) the 2023–2024 budget bills (C-47, C-59, C-69), now essentially all in force (sanctions-evasion STRs Aug 2024, information sharing and new sectors spring 2025, sanctioned-property and discrepancy reporting 1 Oct 2025); (b) Bill C-12 and Bill C-15, both Royal Assent 26 March 2026, which put the 40x penalty increase, mandatory compliance agreements, compliance orders, the "reasonably designed, risk-based and effective" program standard and FINTRAC's seat on FISC in force immediately, while universal enrolment remains passed but not in force; and (c) still-proposed measures: the third-party cash deposit ban and $10,000 cash ban (died-on-the-order-paper-style stall in Bill C-2, re-tabled 3 Nov 2026 as Senate Bill S-8) and Budget 2026's (5 Nov 2026) proposal to move AML supervision of FRFIs from FINTRAC to OSFI.

### Cited Findings

**Bill C-12 — Strengthening Canada's Immigration System and Borders Act (S.C. 2026, c. 4)**
- Royal Assent 26 March 2026; successor to Bill C-2, introduced October 2025, "advanced separately so Parliament could pass the most-agreed priorities" — [Public Safety Canada news release](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-safety-canada/news/2026/03/legislation-that-strengthens-the-immigration-system-and-border-security-receives-royal-assent.html); [DLA Piper, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/03/canada-aml-update-three-bills-make-major-amendments-to-the-pcmltfa); [LEGISinfo C-12](https://www.parl.ca/LegisInfo/en/bill/45-1/c-12)
- **IN FORCE 26 March 2026 — new AMP framework.** Maximums are 40x prior amounts: minor up to $40,000; serious up to $4,000,000; very serious up to $20,000,000 — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/canada-s-new-anti-money-laundering-legislation-significantly-raises-penalties-and-adds-new-violation/); [Torys Quarterly Q4 2026](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/torys-quarterly/q4-2026/canadas-aml-landscape)
- Per-notice cumulative cap: greater of $20M or 3% of gross global revenue for an entity (greater of $4M or 3% of gross global income for a person); affiliated entities measured at group level; "for some very large entities, the cap could exceed C$1 billion" — [DLA Piper, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/03/canada-aml-update-three-bills-make-major-amendments-to-the-pcmltfa); [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/canada-s-new-anti-money-laundering-legislation-significantly-raises-penalties-and-adds-new-violation/)
- FINTRAC must now consider ability to pay (alongside harm and the compliance-not-punishment principle) — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/canada-s-new-anti-money-laundering-legislation-significantly-raises-penalties-and-adds-new-violation/); [FINTRAC AMP changes page](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/pen/3-eng)
- **IN FORCE 26 March 2026 — mandatory compliance agreements.** After proceedings end FINTRAC must require a compliance agreement stating the violation, measures and deadline (extendable up to one year for substantial progress) — [DLA Piper, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/03/canada-aml-update-three-bills-make-major-amendments-to-the-pcmltfa)
- **IN FORCE 26 March 2026 — compliance orders.** Issued if an entity refuses an agreement (deemed refusal after six months) or fails to comply; orders must be made public; contravention is a "compliance order violation" with a penalty of the greater of $30M or 3% of gross global revenue for an entity (greater of $5M or 3% for a person) — [DLA Piper, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/03/canada-aml-update-three-bills-make-major-amendments-to-the-pcmltfa). Note: Torys states the compliance-order figure as "greater of $20,000,000 or 3%" — [Torys Q4 2026](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/torys-quarterly/q4-2026/canadas-aml-landscape) (conflict between secondary sources; verify against the statute).
- **IN FORCE 26 March 2026 — compliance program standard.** Programs must be "reasonably designed, risk-based and effective"; failure is a new "very serious" violation, and all compliance-program requirements (policies and procedures, risk assessment, effectiveness testing) move from serious to very serious — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/canada-s-new-anti-money-laundering-legislation-significantly-raises-penalties-and-adds-new-violation/); [FINTRAC changes page](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/businesses-entreprises/changes-changements-eng)
- **IN FORCE 26 March 2026 — anonymous accounts.** Prohibition on opening accounts for anonymous clients clarified; a client is anonymous if identity cannot be verified under the Regulations or the name is obviously fictitious — [DLA Piper, 28 Apr 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/04/canada-implements-amendments-to-the-pcmltfa); [FINTRAC changes page](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/businesses-entreprises/changes-changements-eng)
- **IN FORCE 26 March 2026 — criminal penalties raised.** Record-keeping/ID/reporting offences up to $2.5M summary or $5M on indictment; reporting offences tied to ML/TF/sanctions evasion up to $10M summary or $20M indictable; new offence of withholding material information or giving false/misleading information to FINTRAC; five-year limitation period — [DLA Piper, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/03/canada-aml-update-three-bills-make-major-amendments-to-the-pcmltfa)
- **IN FORCE 26 March 2026 — FISC membership.** FINTRAC's Director and CEO joins the Financial Institutions Supervisory Committee and FINTRAC may exchange supervisory information on FRFIs with the other members (OSFI, Bank of Canada, CDIC, FCAC, Finance) — [FINTRAC changes page](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/businesses-entreprises/changes-changements-eng)
- **IN FORCE 26 March 2026** — FINTRAC may disclose to the Commissioner of Canada Elections — [FINTRAC changes page](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/businesses-entreprises/changes-changements-eng)
- **Transitional rule:** violations occurring entirely before 26 March 2026 remain under the old policy and amounts; violations on or after that date fall under the new framework; FINTRAC will scope examination review periods to fall entirely within one framework — [FINTRAC AMP changes page (modified 2026-05-06)](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/pen/3-eng)
- **PASSED – NOT IN FORCE — universal enrolment.** New ss. 11.4001–11.4015 require every s. 5 reporting entity (limited exceptions) to enrol with FINTRAC, renew periodically, report changes within 30 days; FINTRAC keeps a public roll and may refuse/revoke for unpaid penalties; failure to enrol is an offence under s. 74. Comes into force on a day fixed by order of the Governor in Council; FINTRAC says the date will be set by regulations published in Canada Gazette Part II — [DLA Piper, 28 Apr 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/04/canada-implements-amendments-to-the-pcmltfa); [FINTRAC changes page (modified 2026-04-13)](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/businesses-entreprises/changes-changements-eng). A 6 Nov 2026 commentary still describes s. 11.4001 as not in force — [ComplyPlus](https://complyplus.ca/blog/budget-2026-osfi-aml-supervision-fintrac-registration-gambling)

**Bill C-15 — Budget 2025 Implementation Act, No. 1**
- Royal Assent 26 March 2026; PCMLTFA changes in force that day except a client-identification clarification deemed in force 1 October 2025 — [DLA Piper, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/03/canada-aml-update-three-bills-make-major-amendments-to-the-pcmltfa)
- PCMLTFA content: s. 36(3.01)(b) disclosure/use rules extended to entities soliciting financial donations from the public; prohibition on disclosing beneficial-ownership discrepancy reports (with exceptions for FINTRAC, police, CRA, ARQ, incorporation bodies); clarification of client-ID regulation-making for real estate, mortgage and title-insurer sectors; all PCMLTFA regulations made on recommendation of the Minister of Finance — [DLA Piper, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/03/canada-aml-update-three-bills-make-major-amendments-to-the-pcmltfa)
- **PASSED – NOT IN FORCE:** Stablecoin Act — stablecoin issuers deemed dealers in virtual currency and must register with FINTRAC as MSBs once regulations are made; Bank of Canada to keep a public registry; plus expanded FINTRAC disclosure to the Minister of Finance on national security/financial-integrity grounds and compliance-information sharing with the Bank of Canada — [DLA Piper, 28 Apr 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/04/canada-implements-amendments-to-the-pcmltfa); [FINTRAC changes page](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/businesses-entreprises/changes-changements-eng)
- C-15 also amended the Bank Act: banks must have policies to detect/prevent consumer-targeted fraud, obtain express consent before enabling prescribed account capabilities, allow customers to adjust transaction limits, and report fraud data to FCAC (in-force dates not stated in source) — [Finance Canada, 6 May 2026](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/05/minister-champagne-highlights-spring-economic-update-measures-to-fight-financial-crimes.html)

**Bill C-2 — Strong Borders Act: PROPOSED, stalled**
- First reading 3 June 2025; still at second reading in the House, last debated 17 September 2025 — [LEGISinfo C-2](https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/45-1/C-2)
- Its un-enacted AML content: offence for any business/profession/charity accepting cash of $10,000 or more (single or prescribed related series; fine up to 3x the amount on indictment); ban on specified financial entities accepting cash deposits from anyone other than the account holder or an authorized person; new Part 1.2 letting reporting entities collect/use personal information disclosed by the RCMP without consent — [DLA Piper, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/03/canada-aml-update-three-bills-make-major-amendments-to-the-pcmltfa); [Blakes on C-2](https://www.blakes.com/insights/strong-borders-act-a-landmark-shift-in-canada-s-aml-penalties/)

**Bill S-8 — Combatting Financial Crimes and Drug Trafficking Act: PROPOSED**
- Introduced in the Senate 3 November 2026 (Sen. Pierre Moreau); at second reading, last debated 17 November 2026 — [LEGISinfo S-8](https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/45-1/s-8); [Bill text](https://www.parl.ca/documentviewer/en/45-1/bill/S-8/first-reading)
- Re-proposes the C-2 cash measures: deposit-taking reporting entities could not accept cash deposits into an account from anyone other than the account holder or an authorized person (prescribed exceptions); general prohibition on accepting $10,000+ in cash (certain reporting entities excluded, further exceptions by regulation) — [BLG, Nov 2026](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/11/bill-s8-proposed-changes-to-canadas-financial-crime-framework); [Torys on Budget 2026](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2026/11/budget-2026-financial-services-and-banking)
- Also: clearer authority for reporting entities to collect/use government and law-enforcement disclosures without the individual's knowledge or consent, with good-faith immunity (PIPEDA amended); FINTRAC mandate extended to sanctions evasion; national-security ground for ministerial regulations limiting or prohibiting transactions, including temporary limits on certain domestic transactions — [BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/11/bill-s8-proposed-changes-to-canadas-financial-crime-framework); [Torys](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2026/11/budget-2026-financial-services-and-banking)
- MSB measures: Minister of Finance may review registrations and direct FINTRAC to deny/revoke on national-security or financial-integrity grounds; criminal-record screening threshold for owners drops from 20% to 5%; MSBs refused PSP registration by the Bank of Canada on national-security grounds become ineligible — [BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/11/bill-s8-proposed-changes-to-canadas-financial-crime-framework); [Torys](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2026/11/budget-2026-financial-services-and-banking)
- Torys' assessment of the third-party cash deposit ban: "likely to require branch operational changes" — [Torys](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2026/11/budget-2026-financial-services-and-banking)

**Budget 2026 (5 November 2026): PROPOSED, no bill tabled as of 6 Nov 2026**
- Annex 3 proposes amending the PCMLTFA and the OSFI Act to transfer AML/ATF supervision of federally regulated financial institutions from FINTRAC to OSFI, aimed at "enhancing supervision and compliance of the largest financial institutions"; Budget Chapter 1 describes OSFI's supervised group as banks, insurers, trust and loan companies and federal credit unions. No transfer date, phase-in, or statement of which agency would issue AML penalties or receive reports — [Torys on Budget 2026](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2026/11/budget-2026-financial-services-and-banking); [ComplyPlus, 6 Nov 2026](https://complyplus.ca/blog/budget-2026-osfi-aml-supervision-fintrac-registration-gambling)

### Inferences
- For Coast Capital the single most consequential in-force change is the March 2026 penalty framework: a compliance-program finding is now a "very serious" violation (up to $20M each), every AMP triggers a mandatory public-facing compliance agreement, and non-performance leads to a public compliance order. Torys notes FINTRAC may treat any violation finding as evidence the program is not "effective."
- Because FINTRAC scopes exams to one framework, any examination with a review period starting on/after 26 March 2026 is assessed entirely under the new regime.
- If Budget 2026 is legislated, Coast Capital's AML supervisor would become OSFI (already its prudential regulator); combined with FINTRAC's new FISC seat (already in force), AML findings and prudential/governance ratings should be expected to converge. Timing is unknown.
- The third-party cash deposit ban (S-8) would require branch/ATM/night-deposit procedure changes and member communications if enacted; it has now been proposed twice (June 2025, Nov 2026), signalling firm government intent.
- Universal enrolment will add an enrolment/renewal/change-notification duty for a credit union that currently has no FINTRAC registration requirement.

### Gaps
- No coming-into-force order or regulation for universal enrolment was found; status as of late Nov 2026 rests on FINTRAC's April 2026 page and a Nov 2026 secondary source.
- Bill S-8 coming-into-force provisions were not confirmed from the bill text (one single-source report says the unlicensed crypto-ATM offence would apply 18 months after Royal Assent).
- No Budget 2026 implementation bill was identified; could not confirm whether one was tabled between 6 and 26 November 2026.
- Bill C-47 (Budget 2023, Royal Assent June 2023) pre-dates the window; its individual PCMLTFA in-force dates were not re-verified beyond the items listed in sections 2 and 5.
- "FINTRAC supervisory cost assessment": the assessment regulation is SOR/2023-195 (see section 2); its first assessment date was not verified.
- Compliance-order penalty maximum ($30M vs $20M) conflicts between DLA Piper and Torys; statute not checked directly.

## 2. PCMLTF Regulations amendments (Canada Gazette) and in-force dates

### Takeaway
Four regulatory packages define the period: SOR/2023-193/194/195 (published 11 Oct 2023; mortgage sector and enhanced correspondent-banking due diligence in force 11 Oct 2024), SOR/2024-266/267 (published 1 Jan 2025; UN sanctioned-property reporting 1 Mar 2025, remainder 1 Oct 2025), SOR/2025-67/68 (published 26 Mar 2025; new sectors 1 Apr 2025, discrepancy reporting 1 Oct 2025), and the still-awaited regulations to fix enrolment and stablecoin dates. No PCMLTF regulation with a scheduled 2027 in-force date was found.

### Cited Findings
- SOR/2023-193 and SOR/2023-194 amend existing PCMLTFA regulations; SOR/2023-195 is a new regulation setting FINTRAC's cost-recovery (assessment) formula; all published in Canada Gazette Part II on 11 October 2023 (registered 26 Sept 2023) — [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2023/final-pcmltfa-regulations-released/); [Canada Gazette SOR/2023-193](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2023/2023-10-11/html/sor-dors193-eng.html)
- SOR/2023-194 came into force on the first anniversary of publication, i.e. **11 October 2024**: mortgage administrators, brokers and lenders become reporting entities (ss. 64.1–64.6, ID verification s. 102.1, PEP checks, $10,000 cash/virtual-currency reporting); correspondent banking due diligence expanded (records on the foreign institution's clientele, reputation, AML supervision, penalties; ongoing monitoring; risk assessment must cover correspondent relationships) — [Canada Gazette SOR/2023-194](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2023/2023-10-11/html/sor-dors194-eng.html)
- Armoured-car obligations under SOR/2023-193 "will be implemented by July 2024" — [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2023/final-pcmltfa-regulations-released/)
- SOR/2024-266 and SOR/2024-267 (Canada Gazette Part II, Vol. 159, No. 1, 1 January 2025) implement the Budget 2023 / FES 2023 amendments — [Canada Gazette SOR/2024-266](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-01-01/html/sor-dors266-eng.html); [FINTRAC changes page](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/businesses-entreprises/changes-changements-eng)
  - **1 March 2025:** reporting of sanctioned-property information under the United Nations Act
  - **1 October 2025:** strengthened MSB registration framework; agents/mandataries may verify identity of corporations and other entities; real estate unrepresented-party ID; title insurers and private ATM acquirers brought in; sanctioned-property reporting under SEMA and JVCFOA
- SOR/2025-67 and SOR/2025-68 (Canada Gazette Part II, Vol. 159, No. 7, 26 March 2025) implement the Budget 2024 measures; on 7 March 2025 Finance accelerated the trade, cheque-cashing and civil-forfeiture measures from 1 October 2025 to 1 April 2025 — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/canada-accelerates-amendments-to-its-anti-money-laundering-and-anti-terrorist-financing-regime/); [Aird & Berlis](https://www.airdberlis.com/insights/publications/publication/anti-money-laundering-amendments-accelerated--businesses-face-unexpected-compliance-rush)
  - **1 April 2025:** factors, cheque cashers, financing/leasing entities; Part 2.1 import/export declarations with AMP scheme
  - **1 October 2025 (not accelerated):** beneficial ownership discrepancy reporting — reporting entities must report material discrepancies between their records and a federal corporation's registry filings to Corporations Canada where they assess a high risk of an ML/TF offence — [FINTRAC changes page](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/businesses-entreprises/changes-changements-eng); [Gowling WLG 2025](https://gowlingwlg.com/en-ca/insights-resources/articles/2025/changes-to-canada-s-aml-atf-regime)
- Gowling: for the sectors added 1 April 2025, "FINTRAC will focus on engagement and guidance before April 1, 2026" — [Gowling WLG 2025](https://gowlingwlg.com/en-ca/insights-resources/articles/2025/changes-to-canada-s-aml-atf-regime)
- The proposed versions of the 2025 package were pre-published 30 November 2024 (Canada Gazette Part I, Vol. 158, No. 48) with comments due 30 December 2024 — [Canada Gazette Part I](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2024/2024-11-30/html/reg1-eng.html); [Osler](https://www.osler.com/en/insights/updates/department-of-finance-seeks-consultation-by-december-30-on-changes-to-canadas-aml-regime/?pdf=1)
- Pending regulations: enrolment commencement and stablecoin-issuer MSB registration both await regulations in Canada Gazette Part II — [FINTRAC changes page](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/businesses-entreprises/changes-changements-eng)

### Inferences
- A credit union's direct new obligations from these packages: (1) enhanced correspondent-banking due diligence (11 Oct 2024) if it holds correspondent relationships; (2) LPEPR/sanctioned-property reporting (1 Mar and 1 Oct 2025); (3) discrepancy reporting for high-risk federal-corporation clients (1 Oct 2025); (4) the option of agent/mandatary reliance for entity verification (1 Oct 2025).
- Indirect effect: mortgage brokers/lenders/administrators, factors, lessors, cheque cashers, title insurers and white-label ATM acquirers that bank with or partner with the credit union are now reporting entities, so client risk assessments and third-party reliance arrangements should reflect that.

### Gaps
- Exact in-force date for armoured cars under SOR/2023-193 (commonly cited as 1 July 2024) not confirmed from the Gazette text.
- Commencement of FINTRAC cost assessments under SOR/2023-195 (commonly cited as 1 April 2024) not verified.
- Which measure sits in SOR/2024-266 vs SOR/2024-267 and SOR/2025-67 vs SOR/2025-68 was not mapped.
- No Canada Gazette Part I pre-publication of 2026 PCMLTF regulation amendments (e.g., enrolment regulations) was found; I could not confirm a "January 2026 consultation paper" mentioned by one vendor source.
- Nothing found on travel-rule regulatory changes in the window (the travel rule for EFTs and virtual currency pre-dates it).

## 3. FINTRAC guidance, reporting-form modernization, enforcement and supervisory posture

### Takeaway
FINTRAC has shifted to an explicitly tougher posture: a published supervisory framework and a revised AMP policy (6 Aug 2025) that dropped "last resort" language, a record year of 35 notices of violation totalling over $247M in 2025/26, and a new post-26-March-2026 penalty framework. Credit unions are squarely in scope: six credit union/caisse penalties were published between June 2025 and September 2026, including $676,500 against a federally regulated credit union (UNI Financial Cooperation).

### Cited Findings

**Guidance and form changes (FINTRAC notices page)** — all from [FINTRAC notices and advisories](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/notices-avis/notices-avis-eng)
- 2023-12-15: change to public notices for AMPs (backgrounder)
- 2024-02-23: guidance on Ministerial Directive on financial transactions associated with Russia (directive issued 24 Feb 2024)
- 2024-04-08: new Suspicious Transaction Report form implemented (API submission available)
- 2024-06-24: modernized Electronic Funds Transfer Reports and Casino Disbursement Reports implementation completed; EFTR guidance updated
- 2024-06-25 and 2024-08-19: guidance on reporting suspected sanctions evasion in STRs
- 2024-07-31: online MSB registry updated; 2024-10-08: mortgage-sector self-assessment tool
- 2025-02-28: new guidance and form to report terrorist and sanctioned-property information (LPEPR)
- 2025-03-21: updated Ministerial Directive guidance for DPRK and Russia
- 2025-03-26: implementation notices for factors, cheque cashers, financing/leasing entities, private-to-private sharing and beneficial ownership amendments
- 2025-07-09: advisory on transactions related to FATF-identified countries; 2025-07-14 and 2025-11-17: Iran Ministerial Directive guidance updates

## 5. Sanctions (SEMA, JVCFOA/Magnitsky, UN Act) and reporting obligations

### Takeaway
The structural changes are the 2023 "deemed ownership/control" rule, the new FINTRAC sanctions reporting channel (sanctions-evasion STRs from August 2024; LPEPR for UN Act property from 1 March 2025 and for SEMA/JVCFOA property from 1 October 2025), and expanded Global Affairs Canada guidance (financial-sector module, updated through 2026). Listings under the Russia and Iran regulations continued throughout 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Deemed ownership: Bill C-47 added SEMA s. 2.1 (parallel rule in JVCFOA) in 2023 — property owned, held or controlled by an entity that a sanctioned person controls is deemed owned by the sanctioned person; three control tests (legal control, board composition, ability to direct activities) — [Fasken, Aug 2023](https://www.fasken.com/en/knowledge/2023/08/significant-amendments-to-canadas-sanctions-laws); [Dentons](https://www.canadaregulatoryreview.com/canada-significantly-amends-sanctions-laws-adding-deemed-ownership-criteria/)
- GAC guidance on control reportedly updated 13 October 2026: aggregation of minority stakes held by two or more listed persons (even unrelated) may constitute control; de facto control indicators include being the largest shareholder and buyback clauses — [Lexology commentary](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=58e6ea82-7e5c-4f5c-b53f-8d8177670840) (single secondary source; UNVERIFIED against GAC page)
- GAC financial-sector guidance (page modified 19 March 2026): screen against the UN Consolidated List and the Consolidated Canadian Autonomous Sanctions List; check for indirect links via intermediaries, subsidiaries and intermediary banks; for crypto, consider pre-transaction wallet screening and post-transaction tracing; maintain a sanctions compliance program; report suspected evasion to the RCMP; file STRs for suspected sanctions evasion (since August 2024) and LPEPRs for property believed owned, held or controlled by or on behalf of a listed person (no completed transaction required) — [Global Affairs Canada](https://www.international.gc.ca/world-monde/international_relations-relations_internationales/sanctions/financial-sector-secteur-financier.aspx?lang=eng)
- LPEPR timeline: UN Act property 1 March 2025; SEMA and JVCFOA property 1 October 2025; FINTRAC form and guidance published 28 February 2025 and reporting requirements notice 1 October 2025 — [FINTRAC changes page](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/businesses-entreprises/changes-changements-eng); [FINTRAC notices](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/notices-avis/notices-avis-eng)
- GAC's Sanctions Bureau has said guidance will keep developing through 2026 including on the deeming provision; in February 2026 a merger of the Export Controls Bureau and Sanctions Bureau was announced — [Baker McKenzie 2026 outlook](https://sanctionsnews.bakermckenzie.com/2026-canadian-trade-customs-outlook-sanctions/)
- 2026 listings activity: Russia regulations amended effective 19 February 2026 and 12 June 2026, with further amendments SOR/2026-80, SOR/2026-172, SOR/2026-188 — [Baker McKenzie](https://sanctionsnews.bakermckenzie.com/canada-expands-russia-sanctions-on-fourth-anniversary-of-the-war-in-ukraine/); [Canada Gazette SOR/2026-172](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2026/2026-08-26/html/sor-dors172-eng.html); [Canada Gazette SOR/2026-188](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2026/2026-09-23/html/sor-dors188-eng.html)
- Iran: additional listings March 2026 and SOR/2026-179 (August 2026) — [GAC backgrounder](https://www.canada.ca/en/global-affairs/news/2026/03/backgrounder-canada-imposes-additional-sanctions-against-iran.html); [Canada Gazette SOR/2026-179](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2026/2026-08-26/html/sor-dors179-eng.html)
- Syria: February 2026 easing removed 24 entities and added two listing criteria — [Global Affairs Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/global-affairs/news/2026/02/minister-anand-announces-amendments-to-sanctions-related-to-syria.html)
- Ministerial Directives under the PCMLTFA (Russia 24 Feb 2024; DPRK; Iran, with guidance updates July and November 2025) add enhanced-due-diligence and reporting duties on top of sanctions law — [FINTRAC notices](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/notices-avis/notices-avis-eng)
- Bill C-29 would make consequential amendments to SEMA and JVCFOA to integrate the Financial Crimes Agency into sanctions enforcement; Bill S-8 would extend FINTRAC's mandate to sanctions evasion — [Osler](https://www.osler.com/en/insights/blogs/risk/canada-takes-aim-financial-crime-bill-c-29-financial-crimes-agency/); [Torys](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2026/11/budget-2026-financial-services-and-banking)

### Inferences
- A federal credit union now has parallel sanctions reporting: disclosure under the sanctions regulations themselves (to RCMP/CSIS) and LPEPR/STR reporting to FINTRAC; procedures should produce both from a single hit.
- Name screening alone is insufficient given the deemed-ownership rule and GAC's aggregation approach; ownership/control data collected for beneficial ownership purposes should feed sanctions screening.

- Belarus-specific measures in the window were not researched.
- No amendments to the text of SEMA/JVCFOA enacted in 2024–2026 were found (other than those proposed in C-29).

## 8. Virtual asset / crypto and MSB rules relevant to banking those clients

### Takeaway
Crypto and MSB clients are now the highest-penalty segment in FINTRAC enforcement and the target of the next legislative round (stablecoin issuers as MSBs, crypto-ATM licensing and limits, ministerial power over MSB registration), all of which raise the bar for a credit union that banks them.

### Cited Findings
- Largest FINTRAC penalties are against virtual-currency MSBs: Xeltox/Cryptomus $176,960,190 (Oct 2025), KuCoin $19,552,000 (Jul 2025), Binance $6,002,000 (May 2024) — [FINTRAC public notices](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/pen/4-eng)

- **PROPOSED (Budget 2026):** FRFIs may not issue stablecoins directly and must use non-financial subsidiaries regulated under the Stablecoin Act — [Torys](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2026/11/budget-2026-financial-services-and-banking)
- **PROPOSED (Bill S-8):** crypto-ATM framework with a $1,000 per-customer limit over any 24 hours, ID verification, record-keeping and reporting; prohibition on operating without a required provincial licence, with liability for persons who knowingly provide certain services to an unlawfully operated machine — [BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/11/bill-s8-proposed-changes-to-canadas-financial-crime-framework)
- The Spring Economic Update (28 April 2026) had announced an outright crypto-ATM ban, citing studies that 85–98% of crypto-ATM transactions are linked to illicit activity — [Finance Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/05/minister-champagne-highlights-spring-economic-update-measures-to-fight-financial-crimes.html)

- FATF 2026: VASP supervision rated moderate; possible licensing regimes for VASPs — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/canadas-2026-fatf-mutual-evaluation)
