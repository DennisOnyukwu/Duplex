## Open banking: Consumer-Driven Banking Act, second-phase legislation, regulator, accreditation, standard body, launch timeline, credit union participation

### Takeaway
Bill C-15 (Budget 2025 Implementation Act, No. 1; Royal Assent 26 March 2026) repealed the 2024 Act and enacted a new Consumer-Driven Banking Act with the Bank of Canada (not FCAC) as supervisor, but the operative regime is not in force: regulations are still only pre-published (27 June 2026), with FDX designated as technical standards body and OBSI as external complaints body on 20–21 November 2026. No data-sharing launch date exists; phase one is read-only, large banks are mandated by a retail-volume threshold, and a federal credit union may opt in through accreditation but is not required to.

### Cited Findings
- The original Consumer-Driven Banking Act was enacted in June 2024; Bill C-15 repealed it and replaced it with a new Act, moving oversight from FCAC to the Bank of Canada, which maintains the public registry and evaluates accreditation applications — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-ca/insights/publications/2026/04/the-new-consumer-driven-banking-act-explained)
- Bill C-15 received Royal Assent on 26 March 2026 — [Parliament of Canada](https://www.parl.ca/documentviewer/en/45-1/bill/c-15/royal-assent); [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/budget-implementation-act-financial-services-highlights/)
- Proposed Consumer-Driven Banking Regulations were pre-published in Canada Gazette, Part I on 27 June 2026 (a Finance release says 26 June); 60-day comment period closed 26 August 2026 — [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html); [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/proposed-consumer-driven-banking-regulations-released-for-comment/)
- Participation "will initially be mandated for specified large banks based on a threshold for retail volume"; mandated banks are not subject to accreditation. The RIAS describes "federal financial institutions not mandated by the Minister, such as banks, credit unions, and insurance companies" as able to opt in via accreditation; provincial credit unions can also opt in — [Canada Gazette RIAS](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)
- Opt-in federal institutions must submit organizational/operational information, a declaration of compliance with security requirements, evidence of technical-standard compliance, and show good standing with their regulator; they may rely on existing OSFI prudential frameworks for security — [Canada Gazette RIAS](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html); [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/proposed-consumer-driven-banking-regulations-released-for-comment/)
- Four accreditation pathways: federal and provincial financial institutions; RPAA-registered PSPs (streamlined); other entities/fintechs (non-streamlined); third-party service providers. Accreditation fee C$2,500 (indexed) — [Bennett Jones](https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Blogs/2026/07/Canada-Advances-Consumer-Driven-Banking-Framework-with-Proposed-Regulations)
- Proposed annual assessments: tiered base fee of $10,000–$150,000 for participating entities by total assets; $10,000 per accredited third-party service provider; estimated one-time capital cost about $10,000 for regulated institutions; total cost $457.7M PV over 2027–2036 — [Canada Gazette RIAS](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)
- Draft operating rules: 99.5% monthly API availability; 24 months of consumer data on request; consent generally lasts no more than 12 months; breach reporting to the Bank of Canada; 5-year record retention; AMPs up to C$10M (entity) / C$1M (individual) — [Bennett Jones](https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Blogs/2026/07/Canada-Advances-Consumer-Driven-Banking-Framework-with-Proposed-Regulations); [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/proposed-consumer-driven-banking-regulations-released-for-comment/)
- Liability "follows the data": a participating entity is liable to consumers for losses from breach of its safeguards; consumers are generally not liable absent gross negligence, with burden of proof on the entity — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/proposed-consumer-driven-banking-regulations-released-for-comment/)
- Phasing: accreditation provisions first, then common rules and assessment fees within one year of final publication; in-scope data phases in by account type (deposit/payment, then lending, then registered and non-registered accounts) — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/proposed-consumer-driven-banking-regulations-released-for-comment/)
- Phase one is read-only. Write access (payment initiation, account opening/closing) is reserved for a second phase subject to consultation; no timing in the regulations. The screen-scraping prohibition is in the Act but will not take effect in the initial implementation — [Canada Gazette RIAS](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)
- Earlier (April 2026) commentary cited a government target of early 2026 for launch and mid-2027 for write access; that commentary itself noted several steps remained — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-ca/insights/publications/2026/04/the-new-consumer-driven-banking-act-explained)
- 20 November 2026: Finance Minister designated Financial Data Exchange (FDX, via a Canada Council) as technical standards body and OBSI as external complaints body; orders published in Canada Gazette, Part I on 21 November 2026. Regulations still not final and will come into force in stages — [Department of Finance](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/11/canada-takes-next-steps-in-implementing-the-consumer-driven-banking-framework.html)
- The Bank of Canada lists a 2 November 2026 consultation on draft supervisory guidelines on accreditation — [Bank of Canada](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/regulatory-oversight/consumer-driven-banking/)
- Bill C-15 also added a PIPEDA data mobility right, operative only where both organizations are subject to a data mobility framework — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/budget-implementation-act-financial-services-highlights/)
- Budget 2026 (5 Nov 2026) proposes extending data portability to telecommunications and air transportation after the Protecting Privacy and Consumer Data Act is established — [BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/11/budget-2026-the-next-phase-of-financial-services-sector-regulatory-reform)

### Inferences
- Coast Capital, as a federal credit union, is almost certainly not a "mandated" participant (the mandate targets specified large banks) and would join by opt-in accreditation. This follows from the RIAS wording, not from a published list of mandated banks.
- With final regulations not yet published in late November 2026 and common rules due up to a year after final publication, live consumer data sharing looks unlikely before 2027. The mid-2027 write-access target from April 2026 commentary looks stale given that the June 2026 RIAS gives no timing.
- The proposed liability rule and 99.5% uptime standard would apply to Coast Capital only if it opts in; members may nonetheless expect parity with large banks.

### Gaps
- The names of mandated banks and the numeric retail-volume threshold were not found (the Gazette page was only partly read).
- No official date for accreditation opening, first data sharing, or write access.
- Recollection, not verified here: the 2024 Act came through Bill C-69 (Royal Assent 20 June 2024) with FCAC as the intended regulator; one search result could not confirm the bill number.
- Whether final regulations were published after 20 November 2026 is unknown.

## Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) and Regulations: registration, supervision, registry, credit union exemption, effect on partners

### Takeaway
The RPAA regime is fully in force: the Bank of Canada has supervised PSPs since 8 September 2025, publishes a registry (including refusals and, from mid-2026, violations), and has issued its first enforcement decisions. Credit unions and banks are excluded entities, but fintech/PSP partners are not, and October 2026 draft amendments would extend the regime to stablecoin payment functions.

### Cited Findings
- The RPAA excludes prudentially regulated entities including banks, authorized foreign banks and credit unions; a PSP that performs retail payment activities for a bank may still need to register — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2025/01/breaking-down-exceptions-and-exclusions-in-the-retail-payment-activities-act); [Bank of Canada registration criteria](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/regulatory-oversight/retail-payments/criteria-for-registering-payment-service-providers/)
- PSPs operating in Canada had to apply in the 1–15 November 2024 registration window — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2025/01/breaking-down-exceptions-and-exclusions-in-the-retail-payment-activities-act)
- Supervision took effect 8 September 2025; from that date PSPs must be registered before performing retail payment activities; the Bank publishes registered PSPs and refused applicants. Not all applications were processed by that date — [Stikeman Elliott](https://stikeman.com/en-ca/kh/canadian-ma-law/bank-of-canada-rpaa-payment-service-providers-registration-update-what-happens-september-8-2025); [Bank of Canada](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/2025/08/refusal-or-revocation-of-registration-under-the-rpaa/)
- First annual reports were due 31 March 2026; PSPs must give 5 business days' notice of significant changes or new activities — [Goodmans](https://www.goodmans.ca/insights/article/retail-payments-supervision---annual-reporting-of-retail-payment-activities)
- The Bank published its first RPAA enforcement decisions in 2026; as of 25 August 2026 all concerned section 23 (performing activities without registration). From June 2026 notices of violation are posted on the PSP's registry entry — [Bennett Jones](https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Blogs/2026/08/Bank-Of-Canada-Publishes-First-RPAApaa-Enforcement-Decisions-Against-Payment-Service-Providers); [BLG reporter](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/08/federal-financial-institutions-legislative-and-regulatory-reporter-june-2026)
- 24 October 2026 draft RPAR amendments: PSPs performing payment functions with "prescribed stablecoins" would need to register and meet safeguarding, operational risk and incident rules; expanded ownership/governance disclosure for all PSP applicants; existing entity-based exemptions for prudentially regulated institutions continue — [BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/11/stablecoin-era-canada-proposes-stablecoin-regulations-and-expands-the-payment-regulatory-framework)
- Budget 2026 proposes targeted RPAA amendments to strengthen Bank of Canada oversight and harmonized consumer protection rules for PSPs with provinces (PROPOSED) — [BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/11/budget-2026-the-next-phase-of-financial-services-sector-regulatory-reform)
- Bill C-31 (Budget 2025 Implementation Act, No. 2), at House second reading 3 June 2026, would exclude RPAA confidential information from access-to-information requests, ban bearer-form documents at federal financial institutions, and require non-discriminatory offering of deposit products in certain cases — [BLG reporter](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/08/federal-financial-institutions-legislative-and-regulatory-reporter-june-2026)
- Unverified third-party count as of 13 August 2026: 1,562 registered, 459 in review, 44 refused, 15 revoked — [private.law wiki](https://wiki.private.law/en/rpaa-canada)

### Inferences
- Coast Capital itself does not register, but its vendor due diligence should confirm each payments partner's registry status, since unregistered activity is the Bank's main enforcement focus and violations are now public.
- Registered PSPs get a streamlined path into both Payments Canada membership and consumer-driven banking accreditation, which increases direct fintech competition for credit unions.

### Gaps
- Current status of Bill C-31 (passed or not) was not checked after June 2026.
- The registry counts are from a non-official source.

## Payments modernization: Canadian Payments Act amendments, Real-Time Rail, Lynx, Payments Canada rules, Interac, fraud liability, confirmation of payee

### Takeaway
The Real-Time Rail went live on 9 November 2026 with eight participants (no Big Six bank and no credit union at launch), mandatory centralized fraud services and confirmation of payee, after Payments Canada membership was opened to PSPs and credit union locals in September 2025. Bank Act fraud amendments are enacted with a planned 1 July 2027 start, and Budget 2026 proposes a $0 consumer liability cap for unauthorized transactions.

### Cited Findings
- Canadian Payments Act amendments took effect September 2025 (29 September 2025 per one source), extending Payments Canada membership eligibility to RPAA-registered PSPs, credit union locals belonging to a provincial central, and designated clearing houses — [Payments Canada](https://www.payments.ca/payments-canada-membership-expansion-providing-better-payment-services-canadians); [NCFA](https://ncfacanada.org/payments-canada-membership-access-reform/)
- First five PSP members announced 27 January 2026 (Wise, Float, KOHO, Paramount Commerce, Brim Financial); 22 new members in the first year, including six on 1 October 2026 — [Payments Canada](https://www.payments.ca/payments-canada-welcomes-payment-service-provider-members); [Payments Canada](https://www.payments.ca/payments-canada-welcomes-new-members-membership-growth-accelerates)
- Canadian Payments Association By-law No. 10 (RTR), SOR/2026-133, made 18 June 2026, in force 24 August 2026; RTR participation is voluntary — [BLG reporter](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/08/federal-financial-institutions-legislative-and-regulatory-reporter-june-2026)
- RTR launched 9 November 2026; designated a prominent payment system effective 8 November 2026 (Bank of Canada oversight); 24/7/365 clearing and settlement on ISO 20022; Interac e-Transfer payments begin to clear and settle on the RTR — [Payments Canada](https://www.payments.ca/canada-launches-real-time-rail-bringing-247-payment-infrastructure-national-economy)
- Launch participants: Wealthsimple, Questbank, Peoples Trust, Paramount Commerce, KOHO, DoBusiness.com, Digital Commerce Bank, Bank of America. More are onboarding "in the coming months, including all major Canadian financial institutions"; RTR expected to reach more than nine in ten Canadians within a year — [Payments Canada](https://www.payments.ca/canada-launches-real-time-rail-bringing-247-payment-infrastructure-national-economy)
- No credit union was a launch participant; CCUA says credit unions will join through phased implementation and Central 1 says it enables clients to participate without building infrastructure — [Payments Canada](https://www.payments.ca/canada-launches-real-time-rail-bringing-247-payment-infrastructure-national-economy)
- Mandatory centralized fraud services at launch: confirmation of payee (consumer accounts on day one), fraud transaction safety score, fraud risk list, fraud reporting platform — [Payments Canada](https://www.payments.ca/canada-launches-real-time-rail-bringing-247-payment-infrastructure-national-economy)
- Bill C-15 Bank Act amendments (ENACTED – NOT IN FORCE): banks must have policies to detect and prevent consumer-targeted fraud, report fraud data annually to FCAC, obtain express consent before enabling certain account capabilities, and let consumers adjust transaction limits — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/budget-implementation-act-financial-services-highlights/)
- Proposed Regulations Amending the Financial Consumer Protection Framework Regulations pre-published 27 June 2026 (comments to 27 July 2026), scheduled to come into force 1 July 2027 with the statutory provisions expected on the same date by order in council — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/proposed-regulations-addressing-consumer-targeted-fraud-in-banking-released-for-comment/); [Department of Finance](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/06/government-pre-publishes-regulations-to-prevent-fraud-and-facilitate-the-next-phase-of-consumer-driven-banking.html)
- Budget 2026 (tabled 5 November 2026) proposes: $0 consumer liability for unauthorized transactions on all bank account types absent gross negligence; a multi-sector anti-fraud framework (financial, telecom, digital platforms); a National Anti-Fraud Strategy; deposit insurance increase to $250,000; easier entry for challenger banks and credit unions; a secondary competition objective for OSFI; Canadian Payments Act statutory review and consultations in early 2027. All PROPOSED — [BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/11/budget-2026-the-next-phase-of-financial-services-sector-regulatory-reform)
- Bill C-15 federal credit union measures (ENACTED): removes the member-vote requirement when a federal credit union amalgamates with smaller provincial credit unions that continue federally, eases asset transactions with provincial credit unions, gives transitional market-conduct relief on continuance; sunset date moved to June 2033; electronic notice-and-access for governance documents — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/budget-implementation-act-financial-services-highlights/)
- Bill C-30 (Spring Economic Update 2026 Implementation Act, S.C. 2026, c. 22; Royal Assent 18 June 2026) gives the Canadian Payments Association good-faith immunity and consolidates Bank of Canada cost-recovery powers — [BLG reporter](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/08/federal-financial-institutions-legislative-and-regulatory-reporter-june-2026)
- OSFI launched a Streamlined Approvals Framework for Targeted New Entrants, including credit unions, on 25 June 2026 — [BLG reporter](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/08/federal-financial-institutions-legislative-and-regulatory-reporter-june-2026)
- Lynx fully adopted ISO 20022; a later Payments Canada rule update removed Swift MT references tied to the November 2025 Swift deadline — [Payments Canada](https://www.payments.ca/bringing-data-rich-high-value-payments-canada-lynx-fully-adopts-iso-20022-standard); [Payments Canada](https://www.payments.ca/payments-canadas-latest-rule-updates-streamline-operations-and-global-alignment)
- Bill C-29 (Financial Crimes Agency Act) was at House second reading in June 2026; the agency is expected to begin operating in fall 2027; Budget 2026 proposes moving AML/ATF supervision of federally regulated institutions from FINTRAC to OSFI (PROPOSED) — [BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/11/budget-2026-the-next-phase-of-financial-services-sector-regulatory-reform)

### Inferences
- A federal credit union is a bank under the Bank Act, so the consumer-targeted fraud obligations (and the proposed $0 liability cap) should apply to Coast Capital from 1 July 2027 if the order in council is made as counsel expect. I did not see a source addressing federal credit unions specifically.

- The Bill C-15 amalgamation and asset-acquisition changes lower the friction for a federal credit union to absorb provincial credit unions.

- No specific 2024–2026 Interac rule changes were found beyond e-Transfer settling on the RTR.
- No Budget 2026 implementation bill number was found; none of the Budget 2026 measures is enacted.
- Whether the order in council fixing 1 July 2027 for the Bank Act fraud provisions has been made is unconfirmed.

## Stablecoins and crypto: Stablecoin Act, CSA rules, OSFI crypto-asset guidelines, CBDC

### Takeaway
The Stablecoin Act was enacted on 26 March 2026 through Bill C-15 with the Bank of Canada as regulator, but it is not in force; draft regulations issued 24 October 2026 target fall 2027. It applies to non-financial-institution issuers, and Budget 2026 proposes that federally regulated financial institutions issue stablecoins only through non-financial subsidiaries under the Act.

### Cited Findings
- Stablecoin Act enacted via Bill C-15 (Royal Assent 26 March 2026); applies only to non-financial-institution issuers of fiat-backed stablecoins; prudentially regulated federal, provincial and foreign institutions are outside the regime — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/budget-implementation-act-financial-services-highlights/); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/canada-enacts-framework-to-regulate-stablecoin-in-bill-c15)
- Proposed Stablecoin Regulations published in Canada Gazette, Part I on 24 October 2026; comments close 23 December 2026; implementation targeted for fall 2027; existing issuers must apply within 30 days of the Act coming into force — [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-10-24/html/reg1-eng.html); [BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/11/stablecoin-era-canada-proposes-stablecoin-regulations-and-expands-the-payment-regulatory-framework)
- Draft requirements: Bank of Canada registration; no interest or yield; redemption at par, generally within three business days; daily 1:1 reserves in high-quality liquid assets held with a Canadian-incorporated qualified custodian; monthly reports with accountant's opinion; no representation that a stablecoin is a deposit or deposit-insured; AMPs up to $1M (serious) and $10M (very serious) — [BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/11/stablecoin-era-canada-proposes-stablecoin-regulations-and-expands-the-payment-regulatory-framework)
- Budget 2026 proposes requiring federally regulated financial institutions to issue stablecoins through non-financial subsidiaries regulated under the Stablecoin Act, and extending the Act to foreign financial institutions not prudentially regulated in Canada (PROPOSED) — [BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/11/budget-2026-the-next-phase-of-financial-services-sector-regulatory-reform)
- OSFI's Capital and Liquidity Treatment of Crypto-asset Exposures (Banking) guideline replaced the August 2022 interim advisory, effective for fiscal 2026 (1 January 2026 for December year-ends); crypto-asset disclosures for banks effective fiscal Q1 2026 — [OSFI](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/guidance/guidance-library/capital-liquidity-treatment-crypto-asset-exposures-guidelines-letter); [OSFI digital and crypto risks](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/risks/risks-arising-digital-innovations-canadian-financial-industry)
- A revised (2027) version takes effect 1 November 2026 for October year-ends and 1 January 2027 for December year-ends — [OSFI backgrounder](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/news/backgrounder-capital-liquidity-treatment-crypto-asset-exposures-banking-guideline-2027-1)
- CSA amendments to NI 81-102 on public investment funds holding crypto assets were published 17 April 2025 and have come into force — [OSC](https://www.osc.ca/en/securities-law/instruments-rules-policies/8/81-102-81-102cp/csa-notice-amendments-national-instrument-81-102-investment-funds-pertaining-crypto); [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/canada/technology/new-csa-amendments-regarding-investments-in-crypto-assets-come-into-force)
- Bill S-8 (Combatting Financial Crimes and Drug Trafficking Act) would affect money services businesses and crypto ATM operators (PROPOSED) — [BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/11/budget-2026-the-next-phase-of-financial-services-sector-regulatory-reform)

### Inferences
- Coast Capital would not register under the Stablecoin Act as an issuer today; if Budget 2026 is enacted, issuing would require a separate non-financial subsidiary. The nearer-term impact is exposure through members, fintech partners and deposit competition.
- The ban on describing a stablecoin as a deposit or deposit-insured is relevant to any co-branded or partner product marketing.

### Gaps
- No CSA instrument or notice from 2025–2026 specifically on value-referenced crypto assets or crypto trading platforms was found; how CSA undertakings interact with the Stablecoin Act is unconfirmed.
- Recollection, not verified here: the Bank of Canada scaled back retail CBDC work in September 2024. The search returned nothing on this.
- The exact effective date of the 2025 NI 81-102 crypto amendments was not confirmed.

## Privacy: successor to Bill C-27, OPC guidance, Quebec Law 25, BC PIPA

### Takeaway
PIPEDA remains the law in force. Bill C-36 (Protecting Privacy and Consumer Data Act), introduced 15 June 2026, is the third attempt at reform and was still at second reading stage per the latest sources (October 2026); it carries penalties up to the greater of $25M or 5% of global revenue and a private right of action. OPC guidance has moved ahead regardless: biometrics (August 2025), age assurance (draft May 2026) and a Children's Privacy Code (20 October 2026).

### Cited Findings
- Bill C-27 died on the Order Paper on prorogation, 6 January 2025 — [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2026/understanding-bill-c36)
- Bill C-36 introduced 15 June 2026 by Minister Evan Solomon; would enact the Protecting Privacy and Consumer Data Act, repeal Part 1 of PIPEDA, and make consequential changes to the Bank Act; most provisions in force on proclamation — [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2026/understanding-bill-c36); [BLG reporter](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/08/federal-financial-institutions-legislative-and-regulatory-reporter-june-2026)
- C-36 content: mandatory privacy management program; privacy impact assessment before cross-border transfers; legitimate-interest basis requiring a PIA; children under 18 with their data treated as sensitive; right to an explanation and human review of automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effect (s. 63); AMPs up to the greater of $10M or 3% of global revenue; fines up to the greater of $25M or 5%; private right of action with two-year limitation — [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2026/understanding-bill-c36)
- A new Digital Safety and Data Protection Commission, with a Privacy and Consumer Data Commissioner, would replace the OPC; its creation depends on Bill C-34 — [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2026/understanding-bill-c36)
- C-36 status: a guide dated October 2026 places it at second reading; no report of a vote or committee referral was found — [BLG guide](https://www.blg.com/-/media/insights/2026/documents/blg-federal-privacy-bill-c-36-guide-en.pdf); [Gowling timeline](https://gowlingwlg.com/en-ca/insights-resources/articles/2026/bill-c36-timeline-of-developments)

## Cybersecurity: Bill C-26 successor (Bill C-8, Critical Cyber Systems Protection Act)

### Takeaway
Bill C-8 received Royal Assent on 15 June 2026 (S.C. 2026, c. 9). Its Telecommunications Act amendments are in force, but the Critical Cyber Systems Protection Act is not: it awaits orders in council and regulations, and as of the latest sources (September–October 2026) no operator classes had been designated. Banking systems (OSFI as regulator) are within the Act's intended scope.

### Cited Findings
- Royal Assent 15 June 2026; Part 1 (Telecommunications Act) in force on assent; Part 2 (CCSPA) in force on proclamation — [Public Safety Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-safety-canada/news/2026/06/government-of-canada-strengthens-cyber-security-and-critical-infrastructure-with-royal-assent-of-bill-c8.html); [LEGISinfo](https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/45-1/c-8)
- CCSPA applies to designated operators of critical cyber systems, including banking systems overseen by OSFI and clearing and settlement systems overseen by the Bank of Canada; penalties up to $15M per violation per day for organizations and $1M for individuals — [BLG reporter](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/08/federal-financial-institutions-legislative-and-regulatory-reporter-june-2026)
- Section 17 requires reporting of cyber security incidents to the Communications Security Establishment within a period set by regulation, not exceeding 72 hours, followed by notice to the operator's regulator; the Justice Laws consolidation shows these provisions as not in force — [Justice Laws](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-47.4/FullText.html)
- As of around September–October 2026, no coming-into-force order had been made and Schedule 2 (designated classes of operators) was empty — [Cybersecurity Canada](https://cybersecuritycanada.ca/news/posts/bill-c-8-law-who-canadas-cyber-security-act-covers/)

### Inferences
- A federal credit union is plausibly a future designated operator (banking systems under OSFI), but designation depends on a class being added to Schedule 2; nothing confirms federal credit unions are or will be included.
- Once in force, CCSPA reporting to CSE would sit on top of OSFI's existing technology and cyber incident reporting, so dual reporting should be anticipated.

### Gaps
- Whether a coming-into-force order or draft CCSPA regulations were issued in November 2026 was not verified.
- No change to OSFI's or FCAC's incident-reporting expectations in the window was found in this session (not searched in depth).
- The empty-Schedule-2 finding comes from a non-official site.

## Artificial intelligence: federal legislation, strategy, OSFI E-23, OSFI-FCAC reports, AMF guideline

### Takeaway
There is no federal AI statute and no AI bill tabled since AIDA died in January 2025; the "AI for All" strategy (4 June 2026) routes AI protections through privacy (C-36) and online safety (C-34) bills. The binding-in-practice items for a federal credit union are OSFI Guideline E-23, effective 1 May 2027, and OSFI's July 2026 bulletin on generative and agentic AI; Quebec's AMF AI guideline takes effect the same day.

### Cited Findings
- "AI for All" national AI strategy launched 4 June 2026 by Prime Minister Carney and Minister Solomon; it proposes modernized privacy law and protections against deepfakes and surveillance pricing rather than a standalone AI act — [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2026/canada-launches-ai-for-all-national-artificial-intelligence-strategy); [CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/canada/2026/06/02/federal-governments-new-ai-strategy-will-emphasize-trust-minister-says/)
- Bill C-36 contains no standalone AI statute; its AI content is the automated-decision explanation and human-review right — [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2026/understanding-bill-c36)
- OSFI published final Guideline E-23 (Model Risk Management) on 11 September 2025, effective 1 May 2027; covers AI/ML models, requires an enterprise model inventory, risk ratings and lifecycle controls, and extends to third-party models under Guideline B-10 — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/osfi-releases-final-guideline-e-23-for-model-risk-management-and-ai-use-by-frfis/); [BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2025/11/osfi-responds-to-the-growing-use-of-ai-key-updates-to-guideline-e-23)
- OSFI issued a bulletin on generative and agentic AI in July 2026, recommending existing secure-development and change-management controls and warning of concentration risk — [McCarthy Tétrault](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/techlex/managing-the-ai-risks-in-the-financial-sector-osfi-s-bulletin-on-generative-and-agentic-artificial-intelligence)
- OSFI-FCAC AI risk report of 24 September 2024 (EDGE principles); FIFAI II report of 23 March 2026 (AGILE framework); both non-binding. FCAC consumer guidance on AI in banking reported for October 2026 — [aibanks.ca tracker](https://aibanks.ca/regulation)
- AMF (Quebec) final Guideline for the Use of Artificial Intelligence published March/April 2026 (sources differ: 30 March decision, 7 April publication), effective 1 May 2027; applies to authorized deposit institutions, financial services cooperatives, insurers and trust companies operating in Quebec — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/quebec-s-amf-issues-guideline-on-the-use-of-ai-for-financial-institutions/); [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-ca/knowledge/publications/27e0daab/amf-s-ai-guideline-is-now-official-what-financial-institutions-need-to-know)
- The Financial Stability Board consulted on Sound Practices for Responsible AI Adoption (12 practices), comments due 22 July 2026 — [BLG reporter](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/08/federal-financial-institutions-legislative-and-regulatory-reporter-june-2026)

### Inferences
- E-23's 1 May 2027 effective date is the hard AI compliance deadline for Coast Capital; the AMF guideline applies only if it is an authorized deposit institution operating in Quebec, which is unlikely for a BC-based federal credit union.
- AI obligations will arrive through three channels: prudential (E-23, OSFI bulletin), privacy (C-36 if passed) and consumer protection (FCAC).

### Gaps
- The OSFI July 2026 bulletin and FCAC October 2026 guidance were not read in primary form; the FCAC item rests on a single tracker site.
- Recollection, not verified here: the Voluntary Code of Conduct on generative AI dates from September 2023, and federal, provincial and territorial privacy regulators issued generative AI principles in December 2023.

## Sustainability and climate disclosure (brief)
